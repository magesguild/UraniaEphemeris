The Inka Khipu Field Manual

A Hands-On Guide to Yupana Arithmetic and Khipu Records

This article is a web edition of the supplied Educator's Field Manual: The Inka Khipu Arcade & Somatic Compute System. It preserves the manual's teaching model while labeling its modern pedagogical system as a reconstruction and adaptation, not as a settled account of ancient Andean practice.

1. Purpose and pedagogical foundations

The manual teaches positional decimal arithmetic, spatial matrix decomposition, and deterministic logic without relying on abstract memorization, external data stacks, or electronic computation.

Its central model is a unified, tactile information-processing architecture:

  • The Yupana is the active workspace: a physical board where counters move through cup receptacles.
  • The Khipu is the durable record: a physical cord archive into which a settled state may be recorded.
  • The clock step is a deliberate manual pass across the board, applying fusion, avalanche, and unweaving rules until the board settles.

The manual calls this somatic computing: the hands, board, counters, and observer together form the computational apparatus. That is a modern teaching lens. It is useful because it makes state and transformation visible, but it should not be mistaken for proof that historical khipukamayuq practice used this exact vocabulary or procedure.

Learning outcomes

  1. Express 0 through 9,999 on a physical four-cup grid, using no more than three counters per row in rested form.
  2. Perform addition and subtraction through fusions, avalanches, and unweaving.
  3. Explore multiplication as spatial loom shifts and division as fair-share allocation.
  4. Explain how a tactile, non-silicon system can process a deterministic workload.

2. Materials and physical setup

For each student or pair: one Yupana board (five rows by four columns) and thirty to forty counters—beans, corn kernels, stones, or tokens. An egg carton or a drawn 5 × 4 grid is enough for a first lesson.

3. Digit representation

The manual's Golden Rule is: always place counters in the largest available cup that fits the remaining value. In rested form, every digit uses at most three counters.

4. Addition and the clock-step avalanche

Set the first number, then place the second number's counters into the corresponding rows without carrying immediately. On the clock step, sweep from units upward:

  1. Two [1] counters fuse into one [2].
  2. A [1] and [2] fuse into [3].
  3. A [2] and [3] fuse into [5].
  4. Two [5] counters fuse and avalanche upward as one [1] in the next row.

For 247 + 385, set 247, drop in 385, and settle the board. The target is 632: hundreds [5] + [1], tens [3], units [2].

5. Subtraction and unweaving

When a row lacks the counter needed for subtraction, remove one [1] from the next higher row and replace it with two [5] counters below. This expresses 10 as 5 + 5. For 1,204 − 531, unweave the thousands [1], remove 531, and settle at 673.

6. Multiplication as a loom

The vertical rows act as loom rails. ×1 replicates a pattern; ×2 doubles it; ×3 triples it; ×5 shifts it upward one row, corresponding to ×10; tens and hundreds shift the whole pattern upward. Place the partial products, then perform one clock step. The worksheet example is 64 × 23 = 1,472.

7. Division and Ayni fair-share allocation

The manual presents division as the inverse of the multiplier loom. It uses Ayni, reciprocal exchange or fair-share distribution, as the pedagogical frame: a communal total is repeatedly partitioned into equal allotments.

  1. Board setup: place the dividend and keep the divisor as a reference.
  2. Test capacity: scale the divisor to the highest occupied tier.
  3. Harvest and allot: subtract one divisor unit and record one quotient count.
  4. Step down: unweave toward lower tiers and repeat.

The manual works through 4,800 ÷ 32 = 150 and 1,472 ÷ 23 = 64 as visible sequences of tests, subtractions, unweavings, and quotient records.

8. The Khipu archive

When a calculation settles on the Yupana, the manual proposes committing its state to a pendant cord. This is an introductory teaching baseline, not a complete account of khipu variation:

FeatureTeaching use in this manual
Single knot (S)Thousands and higher places
Long knot (L)Tens and hundreds; turns give the digit, e.g. L7
Figure-eight (E)A 1 in the units place
Empty cord spaceA zero placeholder for that tier

The manual gives example committed values of 1,701, 14, and 3,001. It also notes that color and other features belong to the wider Andean recording tradition, and that this introductory encoding does not exhaust it.

It does not claim that every historical khipu follows this exact encoding, that these knots form a universal code, or that an archaeological specimen's semantics can be inferred from a classroom exercise alone.

9. Assessment and worksheets

Assess digit encoding, clock-step fusions and avalanches, multi-tier unweaving, Ayni division, and accurate translation from a settled Yupana state to a proposed khipu record. Suggested problems:

  1. 247 + 385 = 632
  2. 1,204 − 531 = 673
  3. 64 × 23 = 1,472
  4. 3,600 ÷ 24 = 150

For each exercise, show the initial placement, every fusion or unweaving, every avalanche, and the final board. The path is part of the lesson.

Provenance and limits

  • Source: supplied PDF, Educator's Field Manual: The Inka Khipu Arcade & Somatic Compute System, English edition, 12 pages, 2026.
  • Preserved: the board topology, cup values, Golden Rule, operations, proposed knot metrics, rubric, and worksheets.
  • Interpreted: “ALU / RAM,” “non-volatile software storage,” “clock-step execution,” “gravitational attractor,” and “database commit” are modern computational teaching metaphors.
  • Open: the historical scope of any Yupana configuration, the semantics of nonnumeric khipu features, and the relationship between this classroom encoding and individual archaeological specimens.

The manual's strongest invitation is practical: put counters on a board, let the state become visible, and let the learner discover that arithmetic can be touched. Its claims become more trustworthy when the line between historical evidence and modern reconstruction stays visible.
